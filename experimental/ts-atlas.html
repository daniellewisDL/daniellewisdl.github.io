Founder so-whatSeed is where the index rose most (+6.5 pts): participating prefs 7→13%, founder warranties 46→62%, consent rights 76→85%. At Series A (−2.3) and C+ (−5.8) it fell; B is roughly flat (+1.2) as higher consent rights offset collapsing participation (17→10%). Negotiate by stage, not by anecdote.
Investor so-whatTerms you take for granted are drifting: investor consent rights are up at B (67→80%) and Seed (76→85%), while board seats fell at A (92→85%) and C+ (92→83%). If you need control, secure it through consents, not seats.

Founder so-whatSeries C+ (valuation +35%, participation −9pp) and B (+2%, −7pp) are founder markets: take the price and refuse participation. Seed is the reverse (+8% price, +6pp participation) — you’re paying for the cheque in structure. At A the typical round costs ~27% of the company; above ~30% you’re off-market.
Investor so-whatA typical round now sells ~14% (Seed), 27% (A), 30% (B) and 43% (C+). At C+ founders are giving up the most equity per round and no longer concede participation (6%). To win late-stage you compete on price and speed; at Seed you can still insist on structure — but 13% participation is already a differentiator founders resent.

Founder so-whatYour investor map must change with the stage: 72% of Seed leads are UK, but at C+ only 24% — and 37% are US. Start US conversations at Series A (US share there rose 12→18%); don’t meet them for the first time at B.
Investor so-whatUK funds cap out around Series B. For a UK investor the follow-on strategy is either to (a) syndicate with US leads earlier or (b) reserve more for A/B pro-rata. Co-invest rates already rose (Seed 26→31%, A 35→39%).

Founder so-whatOutside London, expect VCTs (33% in the North, 28% in the Midlands), EIS funds (28% in the Midlands) and CVCs (18% in East of England). Non-participating prefs drop to 73–77% in the Midlands/North (London: 93%) and East of England sees 1.4× average liquidation multiples. Bring a London or international co-lead to anchor terms.
Investor so-whatRegional alpha: Life Sci & BioTech (78% of £ outside London), CleanTech (59%), Energy (64%) and Hardware (37%) — cheaper entry points (LS avg post-money £66m vs AI £192m) with less syndicate competition. Smaller average cheques (Scotland £5.7m, Midlands £5.0m) favour Seed-A specialists.

Founder so-whatQuote ranges, not points: “Seed participation was 15% in 2023, 7% in 2024, 13% in 2025” lands harder than “13% is the market”. Series C+ founders should time raises for up-cycles — C+ valuations went £211m → £193m → £258m and participation 7% → 15% → 6%.
Investor so-whatTerms you took for granted moved with the cycle: co-investment 49% → 35% → 42% → 33% → 38%, exclusivity 83% → 65% → 74%. Tool 2 · Dilution & exit waterfall

What do founders actually take home?

Model up to four rounds, with market-median UK defaults pre-loaded (Seed £0.59m @ £4.2m post, A £4.6m @ £17.1m, B £17.7m @ £58.2m, C+ £110m @ £258.4m). Change preference types and see how liquidation preferences bend payouts away from headline ownership.

Assumptions: pool fully granted; no further pool top-ups; non-participating holders convert when as-converted beats the preference; participating-capped holders get their preference plus participation up to a 3× total return, and convert beyond that.
